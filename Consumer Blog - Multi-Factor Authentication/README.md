# Got 3 seconds? Trade it to protect yourself from account hackers 99% of the time.

"Are you trying to log in from another device located in ACCRA, GHANA?" 

You stare at your phone. You are on vacation in Florida. 

Does your heart skip a beat when you get an unexpected email alert about your account? Facebook, Google Mail, Instagram, LinkedIn—they all send automated emails that let you know when a new device is attempting to log in. Except this time, you were busy at dinner with your family during a Summer vacation, and you have never even left the country. 

This isn't just a strange notification—it is pointing to danger. Someone, somewhere, is trying to log into your account—and they are halfway there. **Your password is compromised.** 

What do they want access for? Maybe it's to reset your bank password, gain access to your Venmo, or to attack the people in your contacts list by sending emails from your account. 

This spiral of worry is nearly 100 percent avoidable—alright, fine, 99% avoidable—according to experts. 

The secret weapon? **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).** 

MFA is a fancy term for "secondary identity check." Whenever you log in, it requires a secondary verification method to prove that *you* are really trying to log in, and not some scammer in another country who spends all day spinning locks on accounts that lack basic safety procedures. 

Take Gmail, for example. If you log in for the first time on a new device, you shouldn't gain immediate access after inputting a password. Instead, you should have an extra layer of security through Multi-Factor Authentication. It sends a code or notification to another known device—such as your phone tied to your phone number or an authorization application like Microsoft Authenticator—before letting anyone in. 

This step saves you money, stress, and time. The only trade-off is taking an extra three seconds every now and then to sign in. But the payoff is massive: if you get an unexpected notification that someone is trying to sign into your account while eating dinner, you know instantly your information is exposed, and you just blocked a hacker in their tracks through MFA. 

MFA acts as your own personal online bodyguard—free of charge! 

Ensure to turn on Multi-Factor Authentication ASAP with *all* platforms that allow it. Common platforms are:
*   Email servers
*   Social media platforms
*   Banking websites
*   Anywhere you have payment information saved

If you're not sure how to do this, reach out to customer service, your tech-savvy friend, or related help articles on the website for support navigating the settings. Stay safe and take the extra steps. Your peace of mind is worth it.
